Flush or low-flow concentration?

For salty readings (3× or more) at sensors that also measure streamflow. Higher-than-typical flow usually means rain or snowmelt washing salt off roads. Lower-than-typical flow means less water to dilute it, or salty groundwater feeding the stream, a sign of salt stored from earlier storms.

How this works

  • Measure. Specific conductance (µS/cm at 25 °C) tracks dissolved salts. Sodium chloride from road salt raises it sharply after snow and ice storms.
  • Normal level. Each stream's normal is the median of its daily means from May through October, when roads aren't salted (for a past winter, the May–October before it). Streams need at least 60 summer days to be included.
  • Map views. Today divides each day's mean by that normal. Season peak is the highest ratio since November 1, up to the selected day. Days at 3×+ counts the days at 3× or more. Between storms is the low end (25th percentile) of a stream's readings over the same period, which needs at least 14 readings: where a stream sits once storm pulses pass.
  • Salt memory. After a pulse of 3× or more, “days back under 1.5×” counts the days from its peak to the first reading below 1.5×. A stream that stays above 1.5× between storms may be fed by salt stored in soil and groundwater.
  • Salt storms. A sensor “spikes” when it reaches 3× after two days below 3×. A storm is a day when at least 8 sensors (and at least 3% of those reporting) spike in one region, merged with nearby days and regions. Regions: Northeast (New England and NY), Mid-Atlantic (NJ, PA, DE, MD, DC, VA, WV), Southeast, Midwest, Plains and South Central, and West.
  • Flow. Daily mean discharge (parameter 00060) at the same gages, where it is measured. “Typical flow” is the median daily flow over the period shown. Many sensors have no flow gage, and northern gages often skip ice-affected days.
  • Caveats. Data are provisional and can change. Not every spike is road salt: drought, wastewater, mining and sensor problems can also raise conductance. Tidal and estuary sensors are left out. Conductance is not the same as chloride concentration.
  • Data. USGS Water Data API, daily mean specific conductance (00095) and discharge (00060), refreshed every morning. Past winters are archived each May.
